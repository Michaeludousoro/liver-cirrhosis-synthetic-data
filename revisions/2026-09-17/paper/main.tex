\begin{abstract}
\rev{Limited access to complete clinical datasets motivates synthetic data generation, but statistical resemblance alone may not establish predictive value. This study tests the hypothesis that quality-filtered synthetic records improve mortality prediction on held-out real patients with primary biliary cirrhosis. Of 418 Mayo Clinic records, 276 complete cases were divided into 193 training and 83 test patients. Three core models---a generative adversarial network, a conditional GAN (cGAN), and a variational autoencoder---were combined with interquartile-range filtering and cross-model consensus. Additional comparisons included a masked-loss VAE, CTGAN, and SMOTE. Evaluation distinguished distributional fidelity, nearest-neighbour disclosure-risk indicators, and predictive utility under binary classification, three-year classification, and survival modelling. Follow-up duration was excluded from baseline predictors. The masked-loss VAE achieved the lowest tabular Fr\'{e}chet distance (0.062); augmentation increased Random Forest AUC from 0.832 to 0.868, although the paired accuracy comparison was not significant (McNemar $p=0.125$). Synthetic-only cGAN training achieved AUCs of 0.809 and 0.849 for Random Forest and logistic regression, respectively. Consensus filtering produced a 21.1\% near-duplicate rate and did not establish a consistent predictive advantage. Across six synthetic-data methods, the association between fidelity and mean synthetic-only AUC was inconclusive (Pearson $r=0.35$, $p=0.50$). These findings support evaluating synthetic clinical data against its intended task rather than selecting generators by fidelity alone. Fold-contained cross-validation showed no consistent augmentation gain. External cohorts remain necessary to assess generalisability and disclosure risk.}
\end{abstract}
